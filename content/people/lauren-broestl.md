Lauren earned dual B.A. degrees in English and Molecular and Cell Biology from UC Berkeley before joining the Dubal lab as a research associate, where she developed her interest in sex differences in neurologic disease. She subsequently entered the MD/PhD program at Washington University in St. Louis, where her doctoral research in Dr. Josh Rubin’s lab revealed sex differences in glioblastoma radiation sensitivity mediated by p21 and cellular senescence. Now a neurology resident at UCSF, Lauren has returned to the Dubal lab to investigate how pathogenic tau alters X chromosome gene regulation and influences female resilience to neurodegeneration. Outside the lab and hospital, she enjoys reading fantasy, science fiction, and mysteries, solving jigsaw puzzles, and hiking with her dog.
