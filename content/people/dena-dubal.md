Dena B. Dubal, MD, PhD, is a physician-scientist, Professor of Neurology at UCSF, and the David A. Coulter Endowed Chair in Aging and Neurodegenerative Disease. She completed neurology residency, chief residency, and fellowship training in dementia at UCSF. Her laboratory investigates mechanisms of longevity, brain resilience, and female biology that promote healthy aging, advance women’s brain health, and protect against neurodegenerative disease. Her team discovered that the longevity factor klotho enhances brain function and made foundational discoveries revealing how the X chromosome shapes female resilience to aging and disease, opening new avenues for understanding women’s health and developing therapies. Her discoveries are published in leading journals, including Nature, Nature Aging, and Science Advances, and featured in the the New York Times, The Economist, and NPR. Her honors include the UCSF Chancellor’s Award for the Advancement of Women, NIA/AFAR Paul Beeson Award, Glenn Award, Grass Award, and Neuroendocrine Research Award, and she has held leadership roles with the American Neurological Association, JAMA Neurology, JAMA Women’s Brain Trust, Glenn Foundation for Medical Research, and Weill Institute for Neurosciences.
