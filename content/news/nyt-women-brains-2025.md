Researchers found a gene in mice that appears to slow brain aging in females. In aging mice, the silenced second X chromosome partly "wakes up" in the hippocampus, the brain's memory center. Dr. Dubal's team boosted one awakened gene, Plp1, which helps build the myelin sheath around nerve cells. In old male mice, this restored memory and cognition, and in female mice it improved them further. The same gene exists in humans, which suggests a possible route to protecting the aging brain in both women and men.
