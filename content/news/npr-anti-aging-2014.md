Scientists have found that a hormone associated with long life also seems to make people smarter. The gene strengthens the connections between brain cells, a process that's essential for learning. "We've discovered a way to potentially boost cognition," says Dena Dubal, one of the study's authors. That could mean "a very new way to treat diseases," ranging from Alzheimer's to schizophrenia, she says.
